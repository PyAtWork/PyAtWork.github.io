# About

## 🔎 A data scientist with a scientific mindset

My work sits at the intersection of **statistics, machine learning, scientific computing, and complex systems**.

I started in theoretical physics, using Bayesian inference to decipher transitions in complex climate time series. That led to seven years at a technical safety research institute, developing probabilistic methods — Monte Carlo Dynamic Event Trees, adaptive sampling, network-based dependency analysis — to quantify uncertainty in complex engineered systems. Today, as a Lead Data Scientist at BMW Group, I apply the same principles to production systems at scale: time series forecasting, geospatial data quality, and — for the past two years — AI-assisted analytics and agentic prototyping.

I am particularly interested in problems where the data is structured by time and/or space, observations are noisy or incomplete, and uncertainty matters to the decision being made. 

### 💫 I tend to ask

- What structure is hidden in the data? ... and how does the scale of observation change these structures?
- How much of what we observe is signal versus noise? ... or is the truly valuable insight hidden in the noise itself?
- What uncertainty should we preserve rather than hide? 
- Does the model actually improve the decision? ... or is there an entirely different question we should be asking the data?
- Can we make the analysis reproducible and explainable? ... and leave the door open for continuous improvements?

I believe that the challenge in data science lies in the formulation of the actual question of interest posed to the data, just like

> *"If the universe is the answer, what is the question?"*
> — L.M. Lederman
